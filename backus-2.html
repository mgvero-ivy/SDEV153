Backus, MN Pt 2: Cooking

4th of July Weekend 2026

With the five of us to feed, we had quite a bit of cooking to do over the course of the long weekend. Luckily, we had enough fish within the first few days to prepare a nice feast. We were pretty selective about which fish we kept for eating, even though we did not really have to be, as the bag limits for 2026 are very generous for what we were after:

So we had plenty of fish to cook, Most of them were filleted and skinned, but I have a short recipe to share for the smaller panfish.

Easy Grilled Whole Panfish

  • Scale the fish by rubbing a spoon face down against the scales. Remove the head, gut the fish, and score each side two or three times. Make a small cut on either side of the dorsal fin so it will be easier to remove after cooking.
  • Rinse the fish thoroughly and dry them with a paper towel.
  • Heat the grill over a medium flame with the lid closed. While the grill is heating, season both sides of the fish with cayenne pepper, salt, and plenty of lemon juice.
  • Cover the grill grates with foil. Place the fish on the grill, cover, and cook for about four minutes.
  • Flip the fish and cook for another three to four minutes, or until the meat flakes easily from the bone.
  • Remove the fish from the grill. The cuts made near the dorsal fin should make the fin easy to pull out. The fish are now ready to eat.

Fried Fish Tacos with Pico

For the fish fillets we made some phenomenal fried fish tacos. The battering station was pretty easy, just dipped in egg then fully coated in 'Slap Ya Momma' fish fry and fried in vegetable oil for about 3 minutes each side. Topped it in an easy pico (3 roma tomatoes, 1/2 onion, 2 jalapenos, 1/3 cup cilantro, juice from one whole lemon) and served on a warm tortilla. Add chipotle sauce and/or mashed avocado if desired. We managed to eat 2 full days worth of fishing in under a half hour.
